My Dad uses computers and has used them since the early days of personal
computing. He had a Mac at home and Tandy computers at work. His passion
wasn't computers, though; to him they were a tool. But he recognized the
passion I had for them.

He introduced me to programming through an Army learning facility that was open
to dependents on an "as available" basis, where I learned APL in an "online"
course that I took on a teletype terminal connected to an IBM mainframe. When
the Commodore 64 first came out in 1982, my Dad bought one for me.

In a very real way, this application exists because of my Dad (okay, in a
couple of very real ways, but as I write this, I haven't done a subject on
DNA).

I still go to my Dad for advice. We talk on the phone every Sunday, usually for
two to three hours. Occasionally our wide-ranging conversations touch on some
aspect of his time working in the blood bank or the laboratory, and I think
back to the times I got to see my Dad in the lab at Heidelberg.

I doubt this subject will contain much that he didn't already know, but I hope
he still enjoys it.

K.
