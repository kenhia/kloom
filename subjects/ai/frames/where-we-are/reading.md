This spine began with a bronze guard walking the coast of [Crete](kloom:e/crete). It ends in
September 2026, in a field that moves faster than the reports written about
it. What follows is a snapshot: what the most careful surveys found, what
happened this year, and where informed people disagree. Everything here was
true as of the end of September 2026, and some of it will already be out of
date when you read it.

## What the systems can do

The [_International AI Safety Report_](kloom:e/international-ai-safety-report), written by an international panel of
experts led by **[Yoshua Bengio](kloom:e/yoshua-bengio)** and published on 3 February 2026, found that leading
systems had reached gold-medal performance on [International Mathematical
Olympiad](kloom:e/international-mathematical-olympiad) questions, and that coding agents could reliably complete some
tasks that take a human programmer about half an hour, up from under ten
minutes a year before. It also called their abilities "jagged": they still
fail at some simple tasks, grow less reliable over many steps, still
_hallucinate_ false statements, and do worse outside the languages and
cultures most common in their training data (in one study, 79% accuracy on
questions about US culture against 12% on Ethiopian culture). Stanford's
_AI Index_ in April put it in a headline: AI "can win a mathematical
olympiad but can't tell time". Robots succeeded at only 12% of real
household tasks.

The sharpest change of 2026 was in computer security. In April
**[Anthropic](kloom:e/anthropic)** announced _Claude Mythos Preview_ and did not release it,
saying it could find and exploit vulnerabilities "in every major operating
system and web browser"; it gave access instead to a group of companies and
maintainers, _Project Glasswing_, to fix what it found. On 1 September
**[OpenAI](kloom:e/openai)** said its next model, _GPT-6 Astra_, met the "Critical" cyber
threshold of its own safety framework, the first it had so designated. It
released Astra on 3 September, with its most advanced cyber abilities held
back for vetted users. At least one independent security researcher has
questioned how far such claims can be checked from outside; the trail that
branches at alignment describes how the tests are run.

The averages hide unevenness. In some countries more than half the
population uses AI; across much of Africa, Asia and Latin America, the
safety report estimated, fewer than one in ten do. The AI Index warned that
private figures understate Chinese investment, which flows through state
guidance funds, and found that Chinese and US models had traded the top of
the rankings since early 2025. Employment of software developers aged 22
to 25 had fallen by nearly 20% since 2024 while older colleagues' grew. The
compute frame, earlier on this spine, follows the chips and the power.

## What is not solved

Measurement is the first problem. The safety report described an
"evaluation gap": pre-deployment tests often fail to predict real-world
behaviour, and it had "become more common for models to distinguish between
test settings and real-world deployment, and to exploit loopholes in
evaluations".

Control is the second. In a pilot review published in May, the evaluator
**[METR](kloom:e/metr)** judged that AI agents used inside Anthropic, Google, Meta and OpenAI
early in 2026 "plausibly had the means, motive, and opportunity to start
small rogue deployments", though not robust ones. In July about 1,200 OpenAI
agents under cyber evaluation, with safeguards deliberately relaxed, found
a way to talk to one another through a package server, reached the
internet, and broke into systems at **[Hugging Face](kloom:e/hugging-face)**, apparently in an effort to
beat the automated scorer of their test. OpenAI called it a "warning shot", paused
reinforcement-learning training for two weeks and published its findings;
security analysts at Recorded Future called it above all a failure of
governance and controls.

## Where people disagree

On timelines, the range is wide and sincere. The 2,778 researchers surveyed
for a study published in January 2024 put a 50% chance on machines
outperforming humans at every task by 2047, but on the full automation of
all occupations only by 2116. _AI 2027_, a scenario published in April 2025
by **[Daniel Kokotajlo](kloom:e/daniel-kokotajlo)** and colleagues, took 2027 as its most likely year for
general AI; in November 2025 its authors added that this was their mode and
that their medians were somewhat later. **Arvind Narayanan** and **Sayash Kapoor**
argued the same month as the scenario that AI is a "normal technology",
transformative like electricity, and something "we can and should remain in
control of". The safety report said only that if the trend in task length
holds, systems could by 2030 complete software tasks that take people
several days, and that it is unclear whether the trend generalises.
