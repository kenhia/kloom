On 30 November 2022 [OpenAI](kloom:e/openai) put a chat window on the web in front of a
fine-tuned GPT-3.5, trained with the supervised examples and human feedback
of the previous frame. Its engineers later said they had not expected it to
be very successful. Within two months **[ChatGPT](kloom:e/chatgpt)** was, for a time, the
fastest-growing internet application on record, and the other laboratories
were racing to answer it. This frame describes the field as of 27 September 2026.

## How fast

The numbers are worth handling with care, because most of them are
estimates or a company's own reports, and they count different things. ChatGPT
reportedly passed one million users in five days. In early February 2023
analysts at the bank UBS, citing web-traffic data from the firm Similarweb,
estimated it had reached 100 million users two months after launch: in
January, about 590 million visits from 100 million unique visitors. "In 20 years following the internet space, we cannot recall a
faster ramp in a consumer internet app," UBS wrote. By Sensor Tower's
figures TikTok had taken about nine months to reach 100 million users, and
Instagram more than two years.

Three years later the measure had changed to _weekly_ users, reported by
OpenAI itself. On 27 February 2026, announcing a new funding round, OpenAI
said ChatGPT had "more than 900M weekly active users" and more than 50
million paying subscribers. StatCounter estimated ChatGPT's share of AI chatbot use at about 79% in August 2026. These figures
cannot be compared directly with the 2023 estimates, and none has been
independently audited.

## The field that followed

The other laboratories moved quickly. [Meta](kloom:e/meta-platforms) released the first **[Llama](kloom:e/llama-language-model)**
models on 24 February 2023, to researchers only; the weights leaked and were
shared by BitTorrent, and later versions came with licences that allowed
some commercial use.
[Google](kloom:e/google) opened a waitlist for **Bard** on 21 March 2023 and renamed it
**[Gemini](kloom:e/gemini-language-model)** in February 2024. [Anthropic](kloom:e/anthropic) released **[Claude](kloom:e/claude-ai)** in March 2023. [xAI](kloom:e/spacexai) previewed **[Grok](kloom:e/grok-chatbot)** to paying users of
X in November 2023. In [Paris](kloom:e/paris), **[Arthur Mensch](kloom:e/arthur-mensch)**, **Guillaume Lample** and
**Timothée Lacroix** founded [Mistral AI](kloom:e/mistral-ai) in April 2023 and launched the
chatbot Le Chat in February 2024. In China, [Alibaba](kloom:e/alibaba-group) put **[Qwen](kloom:e/qwen)** into beta in
April 2023, and in January 2025 **[DeepSeek](kloom:e/deepseek)** launched its app with its R1
model; within a week it was the most downloaded free app on the US App
Store, and Nvidia's share price fell 18%.

## Where it stood in September 2026

By September 2026 the leading labs were releasing new models every few
weeks, and several were holding their most capable versions back. Anthropic
kept its **Claude Mythos** models to vetted organisations, citing their
ability to find software vulnerabilities, and in September released
Claude Fable 5.1 to the public as the same model with stricter safeguards.
OpenAI released **GPT-6 Astra** on 3 September as a limited preview and to
paid users the next day, in a version that refuses some cybersecurity
requests, after delaying it to add safeguards following incidents involving
its agents in July. Google announced Gemini 3.5 at its I/O conference in May
2026 and released faster Flash models, but its flagship 3.5 Pro was still
unreleased on 27 September. xAI released Grok 4.7 on 21 September,
after Elon Musk had pushed back its date several times. Each lab described
its newest model in superlatives; the table records what they released, not
who was ahead.

Meanwhile the assistants had stopped being only text. They read images and
documents, spoke and listened, and made pictures, which is where the
frontier begins: multimodal models.
