For 130 years the [kilogram](kloom:e/kilogram) was a thing: a small metal cylinder in a vault
outside [Paris](kloom:e/paris). On 20 May 2019 it stopped being one. Every unit of the
[International System](kloom:e/international-system-of-units) is now defined by fixing the value of a constant of
nature, so a well-equipped laboratory anywhere can realize it without
borrowing anyone's master copy.

## The last artifact

The [meter](kloom:e/metre) had left its bar behind in 1960. The kilogram could not, for want
of a method precise enough. Its definition was the **[International Prototype
of the Kilogram](kloom:e/international-prototype-of-the-kilogram)**, made in London in 1879 by **Johnson Matthey** and ratified
as _the_ kilogram by the first General Conference on Weights and Measures
(CGPM) in 1889. It is a cylinder of platinum with 10 percent iridium, about
39 millimeters high and as wide, kept under nested bell jars in a basement
vault at the **Pavillon de Breteuil** in Saint-Cloud, the seat of the
[International Bureau of Weights and Measures](kloom:e/international-bureau-of-weights-and-measures) (BIPM). Opening the vault took
three keys, held separately.

This was the [cubit](kloom:e/cubit) rod's system at its most refined, and it had the cubit
rod's weakness. When the prototype was compared with its official copies, the
masses had drifted apart: the prototype seems to have lost perhaps 50
micrograms over a century relative to them, and nobody could say for certain
why, or which of them had changed. By definition the prototype always weighed
exactly one kilogram, whatever happened to it.

## Fixing the constants

The way out was to turn measurement around. Instead of measuring the
constants of physics in units defined by objects, fix the constants at exact
values and let them define the units. At the 26th CGPM on 16 November 2018
every national representative present voted in favor, and the change took
effect on **World Metrology Day**, 20 May 2019, the 144th anniversary of the
[Metre Convention](kloom:e/metre-convention).

Seven numbers are now exact by definition. The cesium-133 hyperfine
frequency is 9,192,631,770 hertz, which defines the second. The [speed of
light](kloom:e/speed-of-light) is 299,792,458 meters per second, which with the second defines the
meter. The **[Planck constant](kloom:e/planck-constant)** is 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ joule-seconds, which
defines the kilogram. The elementary charge, the Boltzmann constant, the
Avogadro constant and the luminous efficacy of a particular green light
complete the set and redefine the ampere, kelvin, mole and candela.

## Weighing with electricity

Fixing the Planck constant only works if a mass can be measured from it. The
**[Kibble balance](kloom:e/kibble-balance)**, proposed by **Bryan Kibble** of Britain's National Physical
Laboratory in 1975 and renamed for him in 2016, does it in two steps: in one
it balances a mass against the electromagnetic force on a coil, in the other
it moves the coil through the same magnetic field and measures the voltage
it induces. Electrical quantities measured through quantum effects tie the
result to _h_. A second, independent route counted the atoms in
near-perfect spheres of silicon, which fixes the Avogadro constant.

[Eratosthenes](kloom:e/eratosthenes) measured the Earth with a shadow and a distance someone had
paced out. The units are now fixed to constants that are the same in
[Alexandria](kloom:e/alexandria), in Saint-Cloud and anywhere else. From here the trail returns to the
main spine at Eratosthenes, where it began.
