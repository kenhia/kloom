In 1599 a company of London actors, short of a theatre, took the timbers of
their old playhouse across the Thames and built a new one on the south bank
at Bankside. They called it the **Globe**. One of the company's shareholders,
its leading playwright and an occasional actor, was **William Shakespeare**,
then in his mid-thirties, from the market town of Stratford-upon-Avon.

## The wooden O

The Globe was a many-sided timber ring, open to the sky, with three levels of
covered galleries around a yard where, for a penny, the "groundlings" stood
to watch. The stage thrust out into the crowd. There was no scenery to speak
of and no lighting beyond the afternoon sun; the place, the time, the storm
and the battle all had to be made out of words. In _Henry V_ a Chorus asks
the audience to imagine whole armies inside "this wooden O".

Over roughly twenty-five years Shakespeare wrote or co-wrote some thirty-nine
plays and more than 150 sonnets. The plays written in and around the Globe
years include _Hamlet_, _Othello_, _King Lear_ and _Macbeth_.

## New words for new things

Shakespeare wrote at a time when English was changing fast, absorbing words
from Latin, French and the languages of trade, and when printed books were
spreading them. He used a larger vocabulary than any English writer before
him, and many words and phrases are first recorded in his plays — though
often he was writing down speech that already existed rather than inventing
it. What is not in doubt is how much of his language has stayed in ordinary
use: "break the ice", "a wild-goose chase", "in my mind's eye", "the be-all
and the end-all".

More than the words, the plays gave the stage a new depth of inner life.
Characters like Hamlet think aloud, change their minds, and surprise
themselves. Audiences met people on stage who seemed to be working out who
they were.

## Saved by the Folio

Shakespeare died in 1616. In 1623 two of his fellow actors, **John Heminges**
and **Henry Condell**, collected thirty-six of his plays into one large
volume, known as the **First Folio**. About 750 copies may have been printed;
some 235 are known to survive.

Eighteen of those plays, including _Macbeth_, _Twelfth Night_, _Julius
Caesar_ and _The Tempest_, are not known to have been printed before. Without
the Folio, and without the press that made it, they might have been lost as
completely as the Globe itself, which burned down in 1613 when a cannon fired
during a performance of _Henry VIII_ set its thatch alight.
