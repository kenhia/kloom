In the cities of southern Mesopotamia, late in the fourth millennium BC,
scribes began pressing marks into wet clay to keep accounts: grain, livestock,
labour owed. Over the following centuries those tallies grew into
**cuneiform**, a script used for more than three thousand years and for many
languages, from Sumerian to Akkadian.

Writing let a record outlive the person who made it. Law codes, letters,
contracts and eventually literature followed the ledgers.
