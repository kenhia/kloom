In 1418 Florence had a problem the size of a hole in the sky. Its new
cathedral, **Santa Maria del Fiore**, begun in 1296, had been built up to the
base of an enormous octagonal crossing — almost 42 metres across — and the
design called for a dome over it. Nobody knew how to build one that large.

## The design no one could build

The plan, fixed in a model by Neri di Fioravanti decades earlier, was bold
to the point of defiance. It had no flying buttresses, the Gothic answer
used in the cathedrals of northern Europe, which Italian builders regarded
as ugly makeshifts. And the usual way to build any arch or dome — a timber
framework called **centring** to hold it up until the mortar set — was
impossible here: there was no timber long enough and no structure strong
enough to support the whole weight at that height.

In 1418 the wool merchants' guild, which ran the building works, announced a
competition. Two goldsmiths emerged as the main contenders: Lorenzo Ghiberti
and **Filippo Brunelleschi**. Brunelleschi claimed he could build the dome
without centring and, famously, would not say how. He and Ghiberti were
appointed jointly; within a few years the work was effectively his.

## How he did it

Brunelleschi's answer was a set of interlocking inventions:

- **Two shells, not one.** A thick inner dome and a thinner outer one,
  joined by ribs, gave strength without the weight of a solid mass. You can
  still climb the stairs between them to the top.
- **A pointed profile.** The dome rises more steeply than a hemisphere,
  following a curve traditionally described as the _quinto acuto_, the
  "pointed fifth", which pushes outward less.
- **Rings and herringbone brick.** Horizontal rings of stone, timber and iron
  chain resist the outward spread like the hoops of a barrel. The bricks are
  laid in spiralling herringbone courses, so each new course locks into the
  one below and can stand on its own while the mortar cures.
- **Machines.** To lift some 37,000 tons of material, including over four
  million bricks, he designed an ox-driven hoist with a reversing gear, so
  loads could be raised and lowered without turning the oxen around.

The dome was complete to the base of the lantern by 1436, when Pope Eugene IV
consecrated the cathedral. Its marble lantern, also to Brunelleschi's design,
was finished after his death.

## Rebirth

The dome is the emblem of the period later called the **Renaissance**, the
"rebirth" of learning modelled on Greece and Rome. It was not a copy of
anything ancient. It was what people who had studied the ancients could do
next — a technical problem solved with mathematics, craft and nerve.
Brunelleschi is also credited with working out the rules of linear
perspective, which changed painting for four hundred years.
