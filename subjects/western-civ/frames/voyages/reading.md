On 20 September 1519 five ships left Spain with about 270 men under the
command of **[Ferdinand Magellan](kloom:e/ferdinand-magellan)**, a Portuguese captain sailing for the
Spanish crown. Their aim was to reach the [Spice Islands](kloom:e/maluku-islands), the Moluccas, by
sailing west, around or through the Americas. Almost three years later one
ship came home, with eighteen of the original crew aboard. It had gone all
the way around the world.

## Why go west

The islands of the Moluccas were the only source of cloves and nutmeg,
spices worth more than their weight in silver in European markets. Portugal
controlled the eastern route around Africa. A treaty between the two Iberian
crowns had divided the newly contacted world between them along a line
through the Atlantic, and nobody knew exactly where that line fell on the
far side of the globe. If the Moluccas lay in Spain's half, a western route
would make them Spain's.

## The voyage

The fleet crossed the Atlantic to Brazil and worked south along the coast of
South America, looking for a way through. It wintered in Patagonia, where one
captain mutinied and was put down and one ship was wrecked. In October 1520
the ships entered the channel now called the **[Strait of Magellan](kloom:e/strait-of-magellan)**, and
spent more than a month threading its cold, twisting passages; one ship
deserted and turned back for Spain.

Then came the [Pacific](kloom:e/pacific-ocean), far wider than anyone had imagined. The crossing took
more than three months without fresh food; men died of scurvy and starvation,
eating leather and sawdust. In the Philippines, in April 1521, Magellan was
killed in a battle on the island of Mactan.

The survivors burned one ship for lack of crew, reached the Moluccas and
loaded cloves. The **[Victoria](kloom:e/victoria-ship)**, under the Basque navigator **[Juan Sebastián
Elcano](kloom:e/juan-sebastian-elcano)**, then made for home the long way: across the Indian Ocean, around
the Cape of Good Hope through waters Portugal controlled, and north along the
coast of Africa. She reached Spain on 6 September 1522.

## What they found

When the survivors reached the Cape Verde islands on the way home, they
discovered that their careful log was a day behind the calendar kept on
shore. By sailing west with the Sun all the way around, they had lost a day —
a puzzle that would eventually lead to the [International Date Line](kloom:e/international-date-line).

The voyage proved that the oceans were connected and that the [Earth](kloom:e/earth) could be
sailed around, and it showed how enormous the Pacific was. It also cost more
than two hundred and fifty lives, and it opened a century of conquest,
trade and empire whose consequences, for the peoples on the far shores, were
often catastrophic. The globe had become a single, connected, and contested
place.
