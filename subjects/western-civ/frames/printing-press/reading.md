Around 1440 in Mainz, Johannes Gutenberg combined movable metal type, an
oil-based ink and a screw press adapted from wine and paper making. None of
the pieces was new on its own; together they made copying a text cheap,
fast and — for the first time — identical from one copy to the next.

Within fifty years presses were working in hundreds of European towns.
Follow the trail from here into the workshop in Mainz.
