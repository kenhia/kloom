In June 2024 **[xAI](kloom:e/spacexai)**, the artificial intelligence company **[Elon Musk](kloom:e/elon-musk)** founded in 2023, chose a former Electrolux appliance factory in south **[Memphis](kloom:e/memphis-tennessee)** for what the city's chamber of commerce called "the World's largest supercomputer." The company says it built the first stage of **[Colossus](kloom:e/colossus-data-center)**, about 100,000 Nvidia chips to train its Grok models, in four months, against the 24 it says it was told to expect. To run it before its grid connection was ready, xAI parked natural gas turbines on trailers around the building. Whether they needed air permits, and what they added to the neighbors' air, has been argued since.

## The neighbors and the turbines

The nearest homes are in **[Boxtown](kloom:e/boxtown-memphis)**, a historically Black neighborhood settled, as its history is told, by freed people. Within a few miles stand an oil refinery, a steel mill and the **[Tennessee Valley Authority](kloom:e/tennessee-valley-authority)**'s gas plant on the site of its retired Allen coal plant.

In April 2025 the **[Southern Environmental Law Center](kloom:e/southern-environmental-law-center)** (SELC), an environmental law nonprofit, published aerial photographs taken for it that showed 35 turbines on the site, 422 megawatts by its count, and then thermal images in which more than 30 appeared to be running. xAI had applied in January 2025 to keep 15. The **[Shelby County](kloom:e/shelby-county-tennessee)** Health Department held, and xAI repeats, that temporary turbines need no local air permit unless they stay in one place more than 364 days; the SELC called that reading of the law wrong. On July 2, 2025, the department permitted 15 Solar SMT-130 turbines, with catalytic controls to cut nitrogen oxides. By the company's account and the SELC's, the temporary turbines at the factory have since gone.

## Two accounts

**The opponents' case**, as residents, the **[NAACP](kloom:e/naacp)** and the SELC make it: a company built what amounted to a power plant beside an overburdened neighborhood without asking anyone. Memphis already fails the federal ozone standard, the SELC says, and the turbines emit the nitrogen oxides that form ozone, and formaldehyde. "We will not allow xAI to get away with this," the NAACP's president, Derrick Johnson, said in June 2025, when the SELC sent xAI notice of intent to sue on the association's behalf, the step the **[Clean Air Act](kloom:e/clean-air-act-united-states)** requires before a citizen's suit. "We are witnessing their failure," KeShaun Pearson of Memphis Community Against Pollution said of local leaders when it passed.

**The supporters' case**, as the company and the Greater Memphis Chamber make it: the permitted turbines limit nitrogen oxides to 2 parts per million, which xAI calls the lowest-emitting plant of its kind in the country and the Chamber calls 92% below the federal threshold. They back up more than 200 batteries, and xAI says its contract with **[Memphis Light, Gas and Water](kloom:e/memphis-light-gas-and-water)** and the TVA has it leave the grid when the grid is strained. It says it paid $35 million for a substation and "tens of millions" in local taxes, claims no one outside the company has checked. "It's unfortunate that some have chosen to spread misinformation," the Chamber's chief executive, Ted Townsend, said.

Measurement so far is narrower than either case. Air sampling that the city commissioned on two days in June 2025 found nothing at levels of concern, Mayor Paul Young announced; the SELC objected that the wind blew away from the nearest monitors on both days. Environmental health researchers at the University of Memphis modeled a rise in fine particles of about 1%, found no significant change in satellite data, and warned that their work rested on emissions the company reported and that no permanent monitor sits in the nearest neighborhoods.

## Across the state line

Colossus 2, in Whitehaven, began running around the start of 2026, powered from a site about a mile south (by our arithmetic from map coordinates), across the line in **[Southaven](kloom:e/southaven-mississippi)**, Mississippi. Reuters counted 59 turbines installed for it in July 2026, nearly all there. At 80% load, it estimated, 30 of them could emit about 2,500 short tons of nitrogen oxides a year, a projection, not a measurement. Mississippi's regulators held that temporary turbines need no permit, and in March 2026 permitted a permanent plant of 41 turbines. The NAACP sued in April. The Justice Department sided with xAI, but in October 2026 the federal court refused both its request and the company's to dismiss the suit, holding that the plant is not a "mobile source" exempt from permits. The suit continues; a request to stop the turbines awaits a ruling. Under an order agreed with the state in July 2026, the company says, all 69 temporary turbines will be gone by July 2027. The SELC called the order "a stark reversal" of a promise to run them for a year; the company says local air still meets EPA standards.

The promised water plant has slipped too. xAI said it would spend $80 million to recycle 13 million gallons of wastewater a day for cooling, sparing the aquifer that is the city's only source of drinking water. Work began in October 2025, stopped in April 2026, and is to resume by early 2027. How much city water the buildings use is not public; the company says it began with water trucked in. In October 2026 the city council voted 7 to 5 to pause new data centers for 135 days. What the turbines have added to the air of south Memphis and Southaven, no permanent monitor has yet measured.
