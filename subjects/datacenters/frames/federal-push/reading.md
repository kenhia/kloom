A data center on private land, wired to a utility, is approved mainly by a county, a state and a utility commission. The federal government reaches one in three ways: through federal land, through a federal permit (a wetland, an endangered species, federal money) and through the interstate grid. From January 2025 two presidents used all three, in opposite directions on almost everything but speed. This frame follows that push as it stood in November 2026.

## Two orders

| Order                                                                  | Signed        | What it did                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 14141, _Advancing United States Leadership in AI Infrastructure_       | Jan. 14, 2025 | Defense and Energy each to find at least three federal sites to lease for AI data centers with new clean power, running by the end of 2027; developers to plan so that other ratepayers' electricity and water costs do not rise, and for high labor standards such as prevailing wages |
| 14318, _Accelerating Federal Permitting of Data Center Infrastructure_ | July 23, 2025 | Revoked 14141. A "Data Center Project" is one adding more than 100 megawatts of load for AI; with its power lines, pipelines and gas, coal, nuclear or geothermal plant it may qualify for faster reviews, federal financing, federal land, and brownfield and Superfund sites          |

**[Joe Biden](kloom:e/joe-biden)**'s order, signed six days before he left office, tied speed to conditions: clean power, costs kept off other ratepayers. **[Donald Trump](kloom:e/donald-trump)**'s order revoked it and set none of those conditions. It told agencies to find exclusions from review under the **[National Environmental Policy Act](kloom:e/national-environmental-policy-act)**; to presume that federal money under half a project's cost is not a "major Federal action" that triggers one; to move qualifying projects onto the FAST-41 permitting dashboard; and to have the **[Army Corps of Engineers](kloom:e/united-states-army-corps-of-engineers)** consider a nationwide permit for data center work in wetlands. _America's AI Action Plan_, released the same day, said America's permitting rules "make it almost impossible to build this infrastructure in the United States with the speed that is required," and summed up the policy as "Build, Baby, Build!"

## Land, permits and the grid

**Federal land.** In July 2025 the **[Department of Energy](kloom:e/united-states-department-of-energy)** chose four of its sites: **[Idaho National Laboratory](kloom:e/idaho-national-laboratory)**, the Oak Ridge Reservation in Tennessee, the **[Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant](kloom:e/paducah-gaseous-diffusion-plant)** in Kentucky and the **[Savannah River Site](kloom:e/savannah-river-site)** in South Carolina. It asked for proposals at three of them between September and November 2025, for leases the developers would pay for entirely. Separately, SB Energy, a SoftBank company, announced in 2026 what it and the department call the world's largest AI data center, on leased land at the **[Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant](kloom:e/portsmouth-gaseous-diffusion-plant)** in Piketon, Ohio: a claim, not a measurement.

**Federal permits.** How much federal review a data center meets has been measured, at least for wetlands, whose federal reach a 2023 Supreme Court ruling on the **[Clean Water Act](kloom:e/clean-water-act)** narrowed. A POLITICO analysis of 95 data center projects that came to the Army Corps from January 2024 to June 2026 found:

| What the Corps required                                | Projects    | Corps-wide average days, 2024 |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------- | ----------------------------: |
| An individual permit, with public notice and comment   | 26          |                           253 |
| A streamlined nationwide permit, with no public notice | at least 26 |                            55 |
| No permit: the waters were outside federal reach       | at least 27 |                             — |

**The grid.** In October 2025 Energy Secretary **[Chris Wright](kloom:e/chris-wright)** directed the **[Federal Energy Regulatory Commission](kloom:e/federal-energy-regulatory-commission)** to write rules for connecting large loads quickly without raising other customers' bills. In June 2026, rather than one national rule, the commission ordered the six regional grid operators it oversees to show how they would study large loads, guard against shifting costs, allow power plants beside data centers and reward flexible demand. "If new infrastructure is built to accommodate a data center, and that data center doesn't show up, residential customers are not left on the hook to pay the costs," Commissioner David Rosner said.

**Congress.** On November 19, 2026 the Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, 62–33. It narrows legal challenges under the environmental review law and shortens the time to sue. Its ratepayer provision would also let states and utilities charge data centers the full cost of new transmission built for them: "by statute, not suggestion," in the words of **[Martin Heinrich](kloom:e/martin-heinrich)**, a Democratic author. It needs the House, where leaders planned a vote needing two-thirds after Thanksgiving. There Scott Peters of California called it "very responsive to what the country needs right now"; Jared Huffman, also a Californian Democrat, said it would keep communities from being able to "challenge, change, and sometimes stop huge polluting projects."

## The case for and against

**For**, as the administration makes it: building takes years that a contest with China does not allow, and federal land and power plants are assets to use. Supporters of the Senate's bill in both parties add that it would let states and utilities charge data centers the full cost of new transmission. _The race for compute_ follows that argument. The measured permits cut both ways: a minority of projects meets a full federal review, but one that does waits about eight months on average.

**Against**, as critics make it: environmental groups say faster reviews mean less public notice and more gas power. "In any permutation, this bill is a good thing for data centers," said Brett Hartl of the Center for Biological Diversity, who opposes it; the Wilderness Society backed a moratorium on data centers on public lands. A lawyer at the [Southern Environmental Law Center](kloom:e/southern-environmental-law-center) said projects under the nationwide permit's threshold are "effectively rubber stamped." States defended their own power: as it passed the **[One Big Beautiful Bill Act](kloom:e/one-big-beautiful-bill-act)** in 2025, the Senate struck, 99–1, a ten-year bar on enforcing state AI laws. December's Executive Order 14365 asked for a federal law preempting state AI laws, but one that leaves alone state laws on "AI compute and data center infrastructure, other than generally applicable permitting reforms." By our reading of their text, none of these orders reaches a county's zoning vote.

## What is not known

How many projects these changes will speed is unmeasured. Whether any federal site will be leased and built, whether the House passes the Senate's bill, and whether the regional grid rules keep costs off households were all open in November 2026. So was whether a nationwide wetland permit for data centers would be issued. What comes next begins with an older question: _Efficiency and the rebound effect_.
