On 21 July 2026 the **[Food and Agriculture Organization](kloom:e/food-and-agriculture-organization)** and four other agencies published _The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World 2026_. It estimated that 645 million people, between 608 and 696 million, or 7.8 percent of humanity, faced hunger in 2025. That is the third fall in a row since the pandemic peak of 8.6 percent in 2022, but still about 50 million more people than in 2015, when the **[United Nations](kloom:e/united-nations)** set itself to end hunger by 2030. And for the first time Africa holds the largest share: 309 million people, a fifth of its population, against 292 million in Asia.

## How hunger is counted

The headline figure, the _prevalence of undernourishment_, is a model. For each country, FAO's food balance sheets (production and imports, less exports, feed and waste) give the mean dietary energy a person consumes. Household surveys give how unequally it is spread, as a coefficient of variation. The two fix a lognormal curve of habitual intake. Against it FAO sets a minimum dietary energy requirement, the least a healthy person of each age and sex needs for light activity, averaged over the population: 1,838 kilocalories a day for the world in 2025. The share of the curve below that line is the share undernourished.

The plate works an example with invented numbers: a country whose people eat 2,400 kilocalories a day on average, with a coefficient of variation of 0.30 and a requirement of 1,800, has 20 percent below the line, by our arithmetic. Spread the same food more evenly, a coefficient of 0.25, and 15 percent are. FAO's margins of error exceed 5 percent in most cases, and it reports no figure below 2.5 percent.

The second measure asks people. The _Food Insecurity Experience Scale_ is eight yes-or-no questions put to about 1,000 adults a country in the Gallup World Poll: during the last 12 months, because of a lack of money or other resources, was there a time when you were worried you would not have enough food to eat, had to skip a meal, or "went without eating for a whole day"? A Rasch model places questions and people on one scale of severity. Anyone beyond "you ate less than you thought you should" counts as moderately food insecure, and beyond the whole day without food as severely. By this measure 2.1 billion people, 25.8 percent, were moderately or severely food insecure in 2025. One measure infers calories from supply; the other records what people say happened to them.

## Where, and why

Acute crises are classified by the **[Integrated Food Security Phase Classification](kloom:e/integrated-food-security-phase-classification)**, whose top phase, Famine, needs three thresholds in an area: 20 percent of households facing an extreme lack of food, 30 percent of children acutely malnourished, and two deaths a day for every 10,000 people, or four among children. In 2025 it was confirmed in two places, a first in the IPC's history. On 22 August 2025 the IPC confirmed the **[Gaza Strip famine](kloom:e/gaza-strip-famine)**, in Gaza Governorate, after 22 months of war and "a complete halt of humanitarian and commercial supplies in March and April" under Israel's blockade; Israel's government had denied there was starvation, and disputed the IPC's methods. After the ceasefire of October 2025 the IPC found, on 19 December, no area still in famine; in July 2026 it still counted 59 percent of Gazans in Crisis or worse. In Sudan, where the army and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces have fought since 2023, the **[famine in Sudan](kloom:e/famine-in-sudan-2024-present)** was confirmed in the Zamzam camp in North Darfur in August 2024, and in El Fasher, under the Rapid Support Forces' siege, and Kadugli in November 2025; in May 2026 the IPC still counted 19.5 million Sudanese in acute hunger. "Conflict still decides who eats and who does not," said the **[World Food Programme](kloom:e/world-food-programme)**'s emergencies director.

## Enough food

The food supply FAO counted in 2024 was 3,026 kilocalories a person a day, against a world average requirement of 2,377: by our arithmetic, 27 percent more than the average person needs. The share of adults living with obesity rose from 12.1 percent in 2012 to 16.2 percent in 2024, and 2.69 billion people could not afford a healthy diet. **[Amartya Sen](kloom:e/amartya-sen)** put the paradox in the first lines of _Poverty and Famines_ (1981): "Starvation is the characteristic of some people not having enough food to eat. It is not the characteristic of there being not enough food to eat." He built that argument on the Bengal famine of 1943, in the famine trail.

The **[Universal Declaration of Human Rights](kloom:e/universal-declaration-of-human-rights)** of 1948 gave everyone, in Article 25, a standard of living adequate for health, "including food", and the **[International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights](kloom:e/international-covenant-on-economic-social-and-cultural-rights)** of 1966 recognized "the fundamental right of everyone to be free from hunger". In 2015 the world adopted **[Sustainable Development Goal 2](kloom:e/sustainable-development-goal-2)**, whose first target reads: "By 2030 end hunger". FAO now projects that 510 to 520 million people will still face hunger in 2030, 56 percent of them in Africa.
