On 31 July 1948 about 700 people gathered at the Ministry of Agriculture in Moscow for a session of the **[Lenin All-Union Academy of Agricultural Sciences](kloom:e/vaskhnil)**. Its president, **[Trofim Lysenko](kloom:e/trofim-lysenko)**, spoke for the whole first day, on "The Situation in Biological Science". Genetics, he said, was idealist and reactionary, "Mendelism-Morganism" built "entirely on chance", while "science is the enemy of chance." Eight of the 56 speakers who followed defended genetics. On the last day Lysenko answered a note from the floor: "The Central Committee of the Party examined my report and approved it." Everyone understood. Three of the eight recanted at once.

## Cold seeds

Lysenko was an agronomist from a Ukrainian peasant family. At a breeding station in Azerbaijan in the late 1920s he studied how cold governs the growth of cereals, and in 1928 he published the technique he named _yarovizatsiya_, **[vernalization](kloom:e/vernalization)**. Winter wheat is sown in autumn because it will not flower until it has been through a winter. Lysenko moistened its seed and chilled it before sowing, then sowed it in spring like a spring wheat. The plate draws the two calendars. The physiology was real, and old: the Ohio agriculturist John Klippart had reported it in 1857 and the German botanist Gustav Gassner had studied it in detail in 1918. What Lysenko added were promises, and a theory: like Lamarck, he held that what a plant acquires from its conditions of life is inherited by its offspring, the view called **[Lamarckism](kloom:e/lamarckism)**.

In 1929 four leading breeders and physiologists called the method promising in the farm newspaper, and asked for comprehensive tests before it was used widely. Instead it was ordered into practice in 1931.

## How it took power

**[Joseph Stalin](kloom:e/joseph-stalin)**'s forced collectivization of the farms had brought famine: some six million people died in 1932 and 1933. The state wanted quick results, and Lysenko promised them faster than the geneticists could. When his methods failed he blamed "enemies," which suited the terror. **[Nikolai Vavilov](kloom:e/nikolai-vavilov)**, the plant geneticist who had founded the academy in 1929, was told by the commissar for agriculture to give Lysenko every help, and did. In 1935 Vavilov was removed as its president; in 1938 Lysenko took the post. Twelve of the academy's 52 members were shot on false charges in 1936 to 1938. With the ideologist Isaak Prezent, Lysenko named his doctrine "Michurinism," after the fruit breeder **[Ivan Michurin](kloom:e/ivan-michurin-biologist)**, whose work had nothing to do with it. It is now called **[Lysenkoism](kloom:e/lysenkoism)**.

In October 1947 Lysenko wrote to Stalin that genetics served "eugenics, racism, etc." Stalin answered that "the future belongs to Michurin," and he edited Lysenko's speech for the 1948 session himself. Within weeks of the session the higher education minister ordered the dismissal of everyone who had "actively fought against Michurinists." In the autumn of 1948 alone 127 teachers, 66 of them professors, lost their posts; thousands were dismissed or demoted in all. Textbooks were withdrawn and destroyed, and so were mutant strains kept for research.

## The seed collector

Vavilov had traveled in about fifty countries to find where crops began, and built at Leningrad, in what is now the **[Institute of Plant Industry](kloom:e/institute-of-plant-industry)**, a bank of seeds of 187,000 varieties. "We shall go to the pyre, we shall burn, but we shall not retreat from our convictions," he told its staff in March 1939, as Zhores Medvedev's history records. In August 1940 he was arrested while collecting in Ukraine, accused of spying and of sabotaging agriculture, and sentenced to death in July 1941. The sentence was commuted, and he died of starvation in prison at Saratov on 26 January 1943. Through the **[siege of Leningrad](kloom:e/siege-of-leningrad)** his staff kept the collection, replanting it in secret to keep it alive, and did not eat it. The institute's own historians count nine of them dead of hunger; other accounts give at least nineteen.

## The cost, and the fall

Lysenkoism became the official biology of the Eastern bloc, and in China it ruled from 1949 to 1956. Its close planting and deep plowing went into the Great Leap Forward and made the **[Great Chinese Famine](kloom:e/great-chinese-famine)** worse. In the Soviet Union, the historians Svetlana Borinskaya, Andrei Ermolaev and Eduard Kolchinsky write, a generation of biology was lost while molecular biology was born abroad. In 1955 more than 300 scientists signed a letter against Lysenko, and he lost the academy's presidency, but **[Nikita Khrushchev](kloom:e/nikita-khrushchev)** kept him as an adviser. Only after Khrushchev fell, in October 1964, was Lysenko removed from the Institute of Genetics, in 1965, and genetics taught again.

He has defenders now. Some Russian writers recast him as a pioneer of epigenetics, and two biologists, Xiuju Li and Yongsheng Liu, have argued from old trials that spring and winter wheat really were converted. Borinskaya and her colleagues answer that the most prominent such book, Lev Zhivotovsky's of 2014, ignores the facts and corrupts its citations. The lesson of 1948 needs no gene: a science decided by a party's approval stops being science.
